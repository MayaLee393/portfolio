## Houston Rodeo: Journey
I went back to Houston straight after my Software Engineering class, so that I could make it in time for the Journey concert. My relatives from Virginia also came to Houston just to see this concert. We were all big fans of Journey and would always have at least one Journey song played during karaoke.

After waiting for what felt like forever, it was finally time for the concert. We bought one $50 t-shirt and made our way to our seats, excited for the show. I wasn't familiar with the first two songs they performed, but I appreciated the firework and lightshow. The third song they performed was *Don't Stop Believing*&mdash;it's a classic song that everyone knew. However, halfway through the song, the lights and sound suddenly cut off. At first, we thought it was part of the act and the audience sung to finish the verse. Once the verse was over and the lights were still off, we realized that something was wrong. Here's a link to the [video](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1beXV-psDyPPXMg8ywbvYsiTP1_K98YN7/view?usp=drive_link). We stopped believing \:disappointed\:

After waiting for 15 minutes, the technician finally came out and told us there was an electrical fire, and that we should have a good night. We did our walk of disappointment down the ramps of the NRG stadium, and sulked all the way to the car. What a way to start my spring break..

## Guadalupe Peak
Near the end of spring break, my parents and I took a trip to Carlsbad, New Mexico. We stayed at a hotel there for a few days since it was close to Guadalupe Peak. When I first stepped in to the hotel room, I was amazed by how big it was. The room had a kitchen, and even a breakfast table! Unfortunately, we didn't spend much time in the room, since we were either out hiking or eating.

The drive to Guadalupe Peak from the hotel was about an hour long. My dad said the hike would take around 6 hours, so we had to wake up early to get there to make sure we had enough time to hike up and down the peak before it got dark. 

When we first arrived at the park, it was chilly. We decided to where long sleeves, a regular jacket, and a puffy jacket, but 15 minutes in to the hike, we would regret that. Although the air was chilly and there was a breeze, the work from walking up the steps was enough to warm us up.

After 4 hours of walking up steps and climbing rocks, we finally made it to the top of Guadalupe Peak. The air was crisp and the wind almost blew my hat off, but the view was amazing. From up there, you could see miles of the Texas prairies which looked so serene and peaceful. To mark the top, there was a silver pyramid that everybody took a picture with. I found it funny to see advertisements on the pyramid, but no mention of "Guadalupe Peak". 

Finally, the hardest part of the hike came&mdash;the descent. Surprisingly, the descent down felt a lot more painful than climbing up. My feet got blisters and my mom felt the pressure on her knees. Although I could move quicker, the pain could be felt in every step. By the time I got to the bottom, one shoe had a hole in it and the other shoe's sole came off.

Overall, the hike took around 8 hours. I really enjoyed the hike, as I got to see beautiful landscapes, and climb rock structures. Additionally, all the other hikers were extremely friendly. If you plan to hike this alone, I can guarantee that you will find a partner to travel with. Although I enjoyed it, I would not be doing it again. I think that the hike up Guadalupe Peak is a one time event for me, just so I can say "I did that!"
